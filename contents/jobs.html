Jobs

This page helps blind and visually impaired people in India find work. It lists the official websites where government jobs are announced, and explains the rights that make applying and writing exams easier. Everything here links to the official source, so you can check it yourself.

Official websites to check regularly

Government jobs in India reserve posts for persons with benchmark disabilities, and notifications appear first on these official websites:

  • UPSC - upsc.gov.in: the Union Public Service Commission, for civil services and other central government exams.
  • SSC - ssc.gov.in: the Staff Selection Commission, for clerk, assistant and similar central government posts.
  • National Career Service - ncs.gov.in: the Government of India job portal. You can register as a job seeker, search openings and find job fairs.
  • IBPS - ibps.in: exams for clerk and officer posts in public sector banks.
  • Indian Railways - indianrailways.gov.in: links to the Railway Recruitment Boards, which announce railway jobs.
  • State public service commissions and your state’s department for empowerment of persons with disabilities: state-level posts and schemes.
  • DEPwD - depwd.gov.in: the Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, for schemes, guidelines and the UDID card.
  • NAB India - nabindia.in: the National Association for the Blind shares training and employment information for visually impaired people.

Your rights when applying for government jobs

These come from the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 and the official examination guidelines. The links go to the official texts.

  • Reserved posts. Under Section 34 of the RPwD Act, 2016, every government establishment must reserve not less than four per cent of its vacancies for persons with benchmark disabilities. A benchmark disability means a disability of forty per cent or more, as defined in Section 2(r) of the Act, and blindness is covered. Read the Act on the official code website: indiacode.nic.in.
  • Disability certificate and UDID card. Most applications ask for a disability certificate or UDID card. Apply online at swavlambancard.gov.in, or through the Chief Medical Officer’s office or medical board of your district hospital, which processes applications on the same system. Details: depwd.gov.in.
  • A scribe in written exams. The official guidelines of 29 August 2018 allow a person with benchmark disability, including blindness, to use a scribe or reader. You can bring your own scribe (whose qualification should be one step below yours) or ask the exam body to provide one, and you can meet the scribe before the exam. A 2022 update extends this to some candidates below the benchmark disability with a medical certificate. The guidelines and updates are at depwd.gov.in/en/guidelines-2.
  • Compensatory (extra) time. If you use a scribe, you get not less than twenty minutes of extra time for every hour of the exam. Candidates with benchmark disability who do not use a scribe get at least one extra hour in a three-hour exam (in proportion for shorter exams).
  • Accessible ways to write the exam. The guidelines say candidates should be offered, as far as possible, a choice of Braille, a computer, large print, or recording answers. Assistive devices such as a Taylor frame, Braille slate and abacus are allowed.

Practical tips

  • Keep your disability certificate or UDID card ready before you start applying; renew it if it has expired.
  • In every notification, read the section for persons with disabilities first: it lists the reserved posts, the scribe rules and the format for the certificate.
  • Note the last date and apply early; government websites can be slow on the final day.
