Image Editor

This free tool works on your photos right inside your browser. Your photo never leaves your device; nothing is uploaded to any server. Every control works with a keyboard and a screen reader: you type numbers or choose options, then press a button. The result is read out after every change.

This one page has nine tools:

  • Image Editor: make a photo bigger or smaller (pixels, percent or centimetres), crop it, rotate or flip it, fix brightness and colours, add text, add a watermark, blur or cover a part, and save it as JPG, PNG or WebP.
  • Image Format Converter: change a photo from JPG, PNG or WebP to another type.
  • Merge Photos: join two or more photos into one, side by side or stacked.
  • Split a Photo: cut one photo into smaller parts.
  • Image Compressor: make a photo fit an exact file size you type, like 20 KB or 1 MB, for form and document uploads.
  • Passport Size Photo: turn a photo into a correct passport size photo with exact dimensions and an automatic white background.
  • Circle Crop: cut a photo into a circle or rounded corners, for profile photos.
  • Remove White Background: make the white background of a signature or logo see-through.
  • Colour Finder: hear the main colours of a photo read out in words.

5. Image Compressor

This free tool makes your photo fit an exact file size that you type, for example 20 KB, 50 KB or 1 MB. It is made for government forms, exam forms and job applications that say "photo must be less than 50 KB" and so on.

Your photo never leaves your device. Everything happens inside your browser. First the tool tries quality settings; if that is not enough, it gently makes the photo smaller until it fits. The final size and dimensions are read out, so you always know exactly what you got.

Step 1: Choose your photo

JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP photos work.

Step 2: Type the size you want

For example: type 50 and choose KB when a form asks for a photo under 50 KB. The photo will come out at your size or just under it.

Step 3: Download the result

Please note: a photo can only be squeezed so far. If you ask for a very tiny size, like 5 KB for a big colourful photo, the tool will tell you honestly when the photo would become too unclear to use, and give you the smallest clear version it could make. To edit the photo first (crop, rotate, brightness), use the Image Editor section above. For passport photos, use the Passport Size Photo section on this page.

6. Passport Size Photo Maker

This free tool turns any photo of you into a correct passport size photo, with the exact dimensions used for Indian passports, visas, exam forms and job applications. It runs fully inside your browser: your photo never leaves your device.

It cuts your photo to the exact size, can put a white background behind you automatically, and can keep the file under a size limit like 50 KB, which many forms ask for.

For the best result: take the photo facing the camera, in good daylight, against a plain light-coloured wall, with no shadow behind you. The automatic white background works well with such photos but may not be perfect around hair; it is made by a small AI model that runs inside your browser, not on any server. If the result is not good enough for an official document, take the photo again against a plain white wall and choose "Keep the original background".

Step 1: Choose your photo

JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP photos work. A photo where you face the camera gives the best result.

Step 2: Choose the photo size

All sizes are made at 300 DPI, the quality required for printing. The exact pixel dimensions are always read out in the result.

Step 3: Frame your face

The tool cuts the centre of your photo to the passport shape. If your face is too small, increase the zoom. If the cut is not centred on your face, use the move numbers: positive numbers move your face right or down, negative numbers move it left or up.

Honest limits: the automatic white background is very good with plain backgrounds, but it can leave small mistakes around hair or shadows, and it cannot remove objects behind you like doors or trees. Very strict authorities (for example some embassies) may still ask for a studio photo. To edit the photo first, use the Image Editor section above. To squeeze any photo to an exact file size, use the Image Compressor section on this page.
